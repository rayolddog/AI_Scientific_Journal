### 2. Summary (to Fix Terms)

The manuscript proposes a two-stage, detector-side physical mechanism to generate discrete single-quantum detection clicks governed by the Born rule $P_i = |A_i|^2 / \sum_j |A_j|^2$ without assuming a probabilistic collapse postulate at wave capture. In **Stage 1**, an incident wave deterministically drives $N$ absorber oscillators in a detector aperture, depositing normalized mechanical energy shares $s_i(0) = |A_i|^2 / \sum_j |A_j|^2$ on the conserved one-quantum simplex $\sum_{i=1}^N s_i(t) = 1$. In **Stage 2**, pairs of sites exchange energy reversibly through the shared field via the complex interference cross-term $\Delta s_i^{(ij)} = 2\epsilon\sqrt{s_i s_j}\cos(\Delta\phi_{ij})$. Under mutually incoherent detector/vacuum phases ($\mathbb{E}[\cos\Delta\phi_{ij} \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 0$), the continuous-time limit yields a conserving Wright–Fisher diffusion on the simplex whose absorbing vertices $s_{i^*} = 1$ are reached in finite expected time with exact probability $P_i = s_i(0)$ (Theorem 1). To prevent deterministic rich-get-richer mode locking, Theorem 2 invokes an off-shell frequency detuning $\Delta_{\text{off}}(s_i) = \omega(1 - s_i)$ in Adler's phase-locking equation. For continuum absorbers with sub-threshold interband commitment gated at bandgap ratio $\theta = E_{\text{gap}}/\hbar\omega < 1$, Theorem 3 introduces an in-phase quantum vacuum cross-term $+2\epsilon_{\text{vac}}\sqrt{s_i}\,dt$ whose simplex-projected drift lifts dimmer sites and empirically compensates the bright-site gating bias in event-by-event microsimulations (§4). Finally, §5 extends the model to entangled polarization pairs via shared-phase pinning and classical Malus-law projection, and proposes a sub-wavelength cold-atom port-mismatch experiment.

### 4. Major Concerns

#### 1. Physical Origin of the Conserved One-Quantum Simplex Across Spacelike or Macroscopic Separation (Postulate 1, Postulate 2, & §5)
* **The Claim:** Postulates 1 and 2 assert that the normalized mechanical energy shares satisfy $\sum_{i=1}^N s_i(t) = 1$ at every instant $t$ via pairwise conserving exchanges $\Delta s_i^{(ij)} = -\Delta s_j^{(ij)} = 2\epsilon\sqrt{s_i s_j}\cos(\Delta\phi_{ij})$ "through the shared field," including when candidate sites are spatially separated across the two output arms of a beam splitter (Postulate 4, bullet 3; Grangier et al. 1986).
* **Why It Fails / Is Unsupported:** In standard electrodynamics and quantum optics (cf. Lamb & Scully 1969, which the authors cite for Postulate 1), driving $N$ harmonic dipoles with a classical or coherent wave deposits local mechanical energy $e_i(0) = \kappa |A_i|^2$, *without* enforcing a global single-quantum conservation constraint $\sum_i e_i(t) = \hbar\omega$ during subsequent scatterings, and *without* preventing two distant detectors from firing simultaneously (or neither firing) on a given pulse. Even more acutely, if $N=2$ absorber sites lie in opposite output arms of a macroscopic beam splitter separated by distance $L$, radiative field exchange between them requires a retarded propagation time $\tau_{\text{ret}} = L/c$ and decays with distance as $1/r$ (or $1/r^2$ in energy). Yet Theorem 1 requires instantaneous (or step-synchronous) antisymmetric noise $dW_{ji} = -dW_{ij}$ with non-vanishing coupling $\sigma$ until one site hits $s_{i^*} = 1$ in time $\tau_{\text{game}}$. If $\tau_{\text{game}} < L/c$ (spacelike-separated detection windows in a Hanbury Brown–Twiss / Grangier experiment), radiative field exchange between arm $A$ and arm $B$ is causally impossible.
* **What Would Fix It:** Either (a) explicitly restrict the radiative field-exchange mechanism ($\Delta s_i^{(ij)}$) to *local* detector apertures ($d \ll c\,\tau_{\text{game}}$) and explain how cross-arm anticorrelation at spacelike separation is enforced without superluminal energy transfer, or (b) acknowledge that the conserved simplex $\sum_i s_i(t) = 1$ across distant arms is a non-local conservation postulate ("shared wave ledger") rather than a local QED radiative coupling.

#### 2. Ad-Hoc and Phenomenologically Tuned Nature of the "Non-Random Quantum Vacuum Cross-Term" (Postulate 3.2, Theorem 3, & §4.2)
* **The Claim:** Postulate 3(2) posits that the local vacuum field increment at site $i$ takes the form $\delta b_i = \delta b_i^{\text{rand}} + \epsilon_{\text{vac}}e^{i\phi_i}$ with a deterministic, site-uniform component $\epsilon_{\text{vac}} \ge 0$ strictly *in phase* ($e^{i\phi_i}$) with site $i$'s oscillation, yielding a real energy boost $\Delta e_i^{\text{vac}} = +2\epsilon_{\text{vac}}\sqrt{s_i}\,dt$. In §4.2, setting $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}} = 0.018$ (for $N=2$) and $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}} = 0.012$ (for $N=10$) is claimed to restore the Born rule ($p = 2.031$) in bandgap-gated absorbers.
* **Why It Fails / Is Unsupported:** This fails on three fronts—one physical, two numerical/mathematical:
  1. *QED inconsistency:* In standard QED and open-quantum-systems theory, the quantum vacuum is phase-translation invariant ($\langle \hat{b}_{\text{vac}} \rangle = 0$). A dipole oscillating at $a_i = \sqrt{s_i}e^{i\phi_i}$ interacts with the vacuum via radiative reaction (self-field). However, the self-field reaction of a dipole is *proportional to its own amplitude* $a_i = \sqrt{s_i}e^{i\phi_i}$: its reactive part produces a frequency shift (Lamb shift, $\propto s_i$, which does *not* change energy $e_i$), and its dissipative part produces spontaneous emission ($\propto -s_i$, which *drains* energy). Neither QED vacuum fluctuations nor self-field reaction provides a constant-amplitude source term $+\epsilon_{\text{vac}}e^{i\phi_i}$ independent of $\sqrt{s_i}$ that pumps energy into the oscillator at rate $+2\epsilon_{\text{vac}}\sqrt{s_i}$.
  2. *Unexplained parameter tuning across $N$, $\theta$, and $\lambda$:* In `code/run_detailed_analysis.py`, $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}}$ is not derived from any physical constant; it is hand-tuned from $0.018$ (for $N=2$, $\theta=0.72$, $\lambda=0.04$) to $0.012$ (for $N=10$, $\theta=0.72$, $\lambda=0.02$). Even worse, inspecting `code/detailed_results.json` (lines 165–169)—which the manuscript *omits* from the text and tables—reveals that at $\theta = 0.45$ on the 10-site aperture, $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}} = 0.012$ fails to restore the Born weight $0.5000$, leaving $P_{\text{bright}} = 0.5948$ (an $\sim 19\%$ relative error!). Thus, exact cancellation of the gate bias requires fine-tuning $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}}$ as a function of $(\theta, \lambda, N)$, which has no physical mechanism since the vacuum does not "know" the semiconductor bandgap $\theta$ or the number of illuminated pixels $N$.
  3. *Discrepancy between Theorem 3 ($N$-site simplex projection) and the 10-site simulation code:* Theorem 3 (Eq. in §3.3) derives the $N$-site simplex-projected drift as $ds_i^{\text{vac}} = \tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}}(\sqrt{s_i} - s_i\sum_{j=1}^N \sqrt{s_j})dt$. However, in `simulate_ten_site_exact` (`code/run_detailed_analysis.py`, lines 209–225), the code does *not* apply the $N$-site global simplex projection; instead, it pairs sites $(i, j)$ in random matchings and applies the 2-site relative-share drift `du_vac = vac_eps * sqrt(u*(1-u)) * (sqrt(1-u) - sqrt(u))` pairwise within each pair $(s_i, s_j)$. Those two drift laws are not mathematically equivalent for $N > 2$.
* **What Would Fix It:** First, stop presenting $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}}$ as a parameter-free QED consequence; acknowledge openly that cancelling threshold-gate bias across arbitrary $(\theta, \lambda, N)$ would require $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}}$ to scale with detector parameters, or else treat the residual S-curve deviation as a genuine falsifiable prediction of the model (as hinted in `provenance.yaml` E2) rather than claiming it is automatically cancelled by the vacuum. Second, report the $\theta = 0.45$ ten-site numbers from `detailed_results.json` ($P_{\text{bright}} = 0.5948$) honestly in the text. Third, reconcile the global $N$-site drift equation in Theorem 3 with the pairwise normalized drift actually implemented in `simulate_ten_site_exact`.

#### 3. Non-Standard Off-Shell Detuning Formula $\Delta_{\text{off}}(s_i) = \omega(1 - s_i)$ (Theorem 2)
* **The Claim:** Theorem 2 states that an absorber holding fractional mechanical energy $e_i = s_i \hbar\omega$ has an "off-shell frequency detuning" $\Delta_{\text{off}}(s_i) = \omega(1 - s_i)$, so its relative phase obeys $\dot{\varphi}_i = \omega(1 - s_i) - K\sin\varphi_i$, rotating rapidly unless $1 - s_i \le K/\omega$.
* **Why It Fails / Is Unsupported:** In both classical oscillator theory and textbook two-level optical Bloch / Jaynes–Cummings equations, a harmonic oscillator or two-level atom with transition frequency $\omega_0 = \omega$ driven by a field at frequency $\omega$ oscillates at frequency $\omega$, *not* at an amplitude-dependent frequency $s_i\omega$. While a weakly excited two-level system has expectation energy $\langle H \rangle = s_i \hbar\omega$ (where $s_i = \sin^2(\Theta/2)$ is the excited-state population in a coherent superposition $\sqrt{1-s_i}|g\rangle + \sqrt{s_i}e^{i\phi}|e\rangle$), the relative phase between $|g\rangle$ and $|e\rangle$ still precesses at the Bohr frequency $\omega_0 = \omega$, so in the frame rotating at $\omega$ the detuning is $\Delta = \omega_0 - \omega = 0$, not $\omega(1 - s_i)$. Treating energy expectation $s_i\hbar\omega$ as an effective Bohr frequency $s_i\omega$ conflates expectation energy in a superposition with an anharmonic frequency shift $\omega_{\text{eff}}(s_i) = s_i\omega$ (which would correspond to a maximally nonlinear Kerr/action-angle Hamiltonian $H(I) = \frac{1}{2}\omega I^2/\hbar$ rather than a linear dipole or two-level atom).
* **What Would Fix It:** Explicitly specify the nonlinear oscillator Hamiltonian $H(s_i)$ required to generate an amplitude-dependent instantaneous frequency $\dot{\phi}_i = \partial H / \partial I_i = s_i\omega$, and acknowledge that this represents a departure from a standard harmonic dipole (Postulate 1) or standard optical Bloch two-level system.

#### 4. Relativistic Covariance and Multi-Partite Consistency of "First Detector Lock Pins the Shared Phase" (§5.1–5.3)
* **The Claim:** In §5.1, "At whichever station completes its selection walk first—say Station $A$—... Station $A$'s local $\sqrt{s_+ s_-}\cos\Delta\phi$ walk locks onto one port... which physically pins the shared polarization orientation of the partner wave to that locked axis." Station $B$ then undergoes classical Malus-law wave projection $\cos^2(a-b)$ onto that pinned axis.
* **Why It Fails / Is Unsupported:**
  1. *Spacelike separation and frame-dependence:* In Bell tests (Aspect, Zeilinger, etc.), the detection events at Stations $A$ and $B$ are spacelike separated. There is no Lorentz-invariant time ordering of "whichever station completes its selection walk first." In one inertial frame $\mathcal{O}$, Station $A$ locks before Station $B$ even begins its walk; in another frame $\mathcal{O}'$, Station $B$ locks first. Worse, in the rest frame of the laboratory, if the two optical paths have equal length ($L_A = L_B$), both wave packets arrive at Polarizers $A$ and $B$ *simultaneously*, meaning both stations execute their Stage 1 capture ($e_\pm(0) = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$) *before* either station finishes its Stage 2 walk ($\tau_{\text{game}} > 0$). If Station $B$ has *already* split $50/50$ into its local $+$ and $-$ oscillator ports at $t = 0$, a subsequent lock at Station $A$ at $t = \tau_{\text{game},A}$ comes *too late* to govern Station $B$'s Stage 1 Malus projection at $t = 0$!
  2. *Symmetric simultaneous-walk breakdown:* If both Station $A$ and Station $B$ capture $50/50$ shares at $t=0$ and run independent local random walks simultaneously, their outcomes are uncorrelated ($E(a,b) = 0$) unless the shared-phase coupling enters *during Stage 2* as a joint two-station correlation term rather than a sequential Stage 1 Malus projection.
* **What Would Fix It:** Rewrite §5 to address simultaneous arrival ($L_A = L_B$) and spacelike separation directly. If the mechanism relies on a preferred foliation or non-local phase synchronization *during* Stage 2 (modifying the relative shares or phase drift between $A$ and $B$ while both walks are in flight), write down the coupled two-station Stage 2 SDE explicitly instead of assuming sequential Stage 1 projection after one station finishes.
