### Part I — Physical & Foundational Clarifications (Why Resolving the Aperture into Individual Atoms on a 2D Crystal Lattice Solves the Reviewers' Continuum-Gate Objection)

1. **Individual Atoms in the Aperture vs. Calculating the "Integral of the Aperture" (Resolving Gemini Major #2, Grok Major #2, and GPT-6 Major #6 & #7):**  
   - *Why the Reviewers Found a Backward-ODE Obstruction and a $\theta = 0.45$ Breakdown:* In Stage 01, the baseline simulation lumped each detector port into a **single macroscopic variable $x = S \in [0, 1]$** (the integral of the aperture). When GPT-6 wrote the 1D backward Kolmogorov equation $\frac{\sigma^2}{2}x(1-x)q''(x) + b(x)q'(x) + r_1(x)(1-q) - r_2(x)q = 0$, it assumed a 2-atom detector where Port 1's single site starts at $x = S$. Whenever $S > \theta$ (e.g., $S = 0.80 > \theta = 0.72$, or the lumped 10-site bright port with $s_0(0) = 0.50 > \theta = 0.45$), that single lumped site is **already above the semiconductor bandgap threshold at $t = 0$ before a single step of the random walk occurs!**
   - *What Happens When the Aperture Is Resolved into Individual Atoms (`run_atom_by_atom_aperture.py`) and a 32-Atom 2D Condensed-Matter Crystal Lattice (`run_crystal_lattice_simulation.py`):* In a real detector, an interference fringe or port illuminates **many individual absorber atoms or lattice electrons** ($M_1 \gg 1$ atoms in Port 1, $M_2 \gg 1$ atoms in Port 2). Even at $S = 0.90$, across $M_1 = 10$ or $16$ individual lattice atoms, **every single atom starts at $s_m(0) \approx S/M_1 \sim 0.05\text{–}0.09 \ll \theta = 0.45 < 0.72$**. Not a single atom is above the threshold gate at $t = 0$! Because the total port share $S(t) = \sum_{m=1}^{M_1} s_m(t)$ is a conserved martingale throughout the entire sub-threshold consolidation phase ($\max_m s_m(t) \le \theta$), by the time nearest-neighbor exciton hopping on the 2D crystal lattice and shared-vacuum exchange consolidate enough energy onto a single dominant atom to cross $\theta$, which port wins has already been decided fairly:
     - **20-Atom Aperture at $\theta = 0.72$ (off-center random alignment $\delta x_0 \sim \mathcal{U}(-0.5, +0.5)a_{\text{lat}}$, $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}} = 0$):** $\hat{p} = 2.0238 \pm 0.0135$, RMS error **$0.49$ percentage points**, $\chi^2_{\text{Born}} = 12.92$ ($\text{dof}=9$, $p_{\text{val}} = 0.166$ — statistically consistent with the exact Born rule), with tight ($<2\%$) bright-spot tie rates reduced $2.8\times$ ($64.15\% \to 22.56\%$).
     - **20-Atom Aperture at $\theta = 0.45$ (off-center random alignment, $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}} = 0.006$):** $\hat{p} = 2.0324 \pm 0.0135$, RMS error **$0.46$ percentage points**, $\chi^2_{\text{Born}} = 11.91$ ($\text{dof}=9$, $p_{\text{val}} = 0.219$ — statistically consistent with the exact Born rule).
     - **50/50 Bright-Fringe Test at $\theta = 0.45$:** Resolving the bright fringe from $1$ lumped site ($P_{\text{bright}} = 0.5959$) into $10$ individual atoms (vs. $10$ dim atoms) restores $\widehat{P}_{\text{bright}} = \mathbf{0.5002}$ (and $\mathbf{0.5082}$ for $5$ bright vs. $15$ dim atoms; $\mathbf{0.4981}$ on the $32$-atom 2D crystal lattice!).
     - **32-Atom 2D Crystal Lattice ($4\times 4$ square lattice per patch, off-center random alignment $\delta\mathbf{r}_0 \sim \mathcal{U}(-0.5, +0.5)a_{\text{lat}}$, $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}} = 0.005$):** Tight ($<2\%$) bright-spot tie rate drops $6.6\times$ ($80.88\% \to 12.32\%$) and mean selection steps at $\theta=0.45$ drop by $45\%$ ($524 \to 288$ steps); $\hat{p} = 1.9887 \pm 0.0133$, RMS error **$0.43$ percentage points**, $\chi^2_{\text{Born}} = 10.42$ ($\text{dof}=9$, $p_{\text{val}} = 0.317$) at $\theta = 0.72$, and $\hat{p} = 2.0407 \pm 0.0135$, RMS error **$0.47$ percentage points**, $\chi^2_{\text{Born}} = 15.22$ ($\text{dof}=9$, $p_{\text{val}} = 0.085 > 0.05$) at $\theta = 0.45$ with the **same unretuned $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}} = 0.005$** (and $\chi^2_{\text{Born}} = 8.64, p_{\text{val}} = 0.471$ at $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}} = 0.006$)!

2. **Pre-Modulus Phase Coupling and Surface-Conditioned Quantum Vacuum Phase (Addressing Gemini Major #2, Grok Major #1 & #2, GPT-6 Major #5 & #6):**  
   - **Harmonic Oscillator Calculation Predicts Interference, Not Pre-Packaged Fractional Particles:** We clarify in §1.1 and §2.1 that the Stage-1 linear oscillator / wave calculation predicts the complex interference pattern $A_m = |A_m|e^{i\phi^{\text{inc}}_m}$ across the aperture, not pre-packaged mechanical particle fragments.
   - **Why the Quantum Vacuum Phase at a Detector Surface Is Not Free-Space White Noise:** At a condensed-matter detector surface, Casimir–Polder boundary reflection phase pinning [Casimir & Polder 1948; Sukenik et al. 1993; Perreault & Cronin 2005] and the applied detector bias electric field $\mathbf{E}_{\text{bias}}$ narrow the virtual vacuum phase $\phi^{\text{vac}}_m$ into a **surface-conditioned von Mises distribution** ($\kappa_{\text{surf}} > 0$) before the incident particle arrives.
   - **Three-Way Pre-Modulus Phase Alignment & Dominant-Atom Vacuum Pulling:** The phases of the incident wave ($\phi^{\text{inc}}_m$), the surface-conditioned virtual vacuum ($\phi^{\text{vac}}_m$), and the individual detector atom ($\phi^{\text{det}}_m$) couple **before** any modulus squared is calculated. The atom whose phase aligns closest with the incident particle and virtual vacuum particle pulls the local vacuum phase into lock and becomes the **dominant absorbing atom**.

3. **Spacelike Nonlocality Is Entailed by the $\psi$-Ontic Postulate (Contesting Gemini Major #1 & #4, Grok Major #4, GPT-6 Major #9):**  
   - Because the framework explicitly postulates that the wavefunction $\psi$ is a **real physical property ($\psi$-ontic)** in a single-world universe, **spacelike nonlocality is a mandatory mathematical consequence of Bell's theorem [Bell 1964] and the PBR theorem [Pusey, Barrett & Rudolph 2012]**, not a defect. Two beam-splitter arms or entangled waves are lobes of a single extended $\psi$-ontic wave object.

1. **Elimination of the $O(\epsilon^2)$ Equalizing Drift $(s_j - s_i)\sin^2\epsilon$ (GPT-6 Major #1):**
