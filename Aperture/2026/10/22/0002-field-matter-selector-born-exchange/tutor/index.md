## Level 1 — For a Non-Specialist

When a single particle of light (a photon) travels through a double-slit apparatus and hits a camera sensor made of millions of atoms, the light wave spreads across the entire sensor. Yet we never see a million atoms each light up by one-millionth of a flash—instead, **one single atom** absorbs the entire packet of energy and produces a click. Repeating the experiment thousands of times builds up the famous wave interference pattern, where the chance of an atom clicking is proportional to the brightness (squared wave amplitude $|A_i|^2$) at that atom's location. Standard quantum mechanics treats this jump from a spread-out wave to a single click as a fundamental rule (the **Born rule**) without describing how the atoms in the sensor decide which one gets the whole packet.

This paper proposes and simulates a physical mechanism for how the atoms in the detector settle that competition:
1. **Stage 1 (Wave Capture):** When the wave arrives, every illuminated atom starts vibrating with mechanical energy proportional to the local wave brightness $|A_i|^2$—just like a hologram recording an interference pattern. At this stage, no probability is involved; every atom holds a small fraction $s_i(0)$ of one quantum of energy, adding up to $100\%$ across the whole detector ($\sum_i s_i = 1$).
2. **Stage 2 (Fair Tug-of-War):** Because no single atom has enough energy to complete a quantum jump on its own, the vibrating atoms exchange energy back and forth through the shared electromagnetic field. When two oscillating waves overlap, the energy transferred between them is their **wave-interference cross-term**, $2\sqrt{s_i s_j}\cos(\Delta\phi_{ij})$. Because the atoms in a room-temperature or cryogenic detector have random, uncoordinated vibration phases $\Delta\phi_{ij}$, $\cos(\Delta\phi_{ij})$ is equally likely to be positive or negative at each step.
3. **Why Brightness Becomes Probability:** A basic theorem of probability (the **martingale stopping theorem**—the same math that says in a fair coin-flipping game where you start with $30\%$ of the chips on the table and play until one person holds $100\%$ of the chips, your chance of winning is exactly $30\%$) guarantees that an atom starting with energy fraction $s_i(0) = |A_i|^2 / \sum_j |A_j|^2$ wins the entire quantum in exactly that fraction of trials.

## Level 2 — For an Adjacent-Field Scientist

This paper addresses the **single-outcome selection problem** in semiclassical/ψ-ontic detector models by combining three mathematical mechanisms:
1. **Wright–Fisher Simplex Martingale from Linear Wave Mixing (Theorem 1):** Representing each absorber site's excitation share $s_i(t) = e_i(t)/\hbar\omega$ via a complex oscillator amplitude $a_i = \sqrt{s_i}e^{i\phi_i}$, pairwise unitary field mixing $a_i' = a_i\cos\epsilon + a_j e^{i\xi_{ij}}\sin\epsilon$ changes the site energies by the interference cross-term $\Delta s_i^{(ij)} = -\Delta s_j^{(ij)} = 2\epsilon\sqrt{s_i s_j}\cos(\Delta\phi_{ij})$. Under rapid phase decoherence ($\Gamma_{\text{rad}}/\Gamma_{\text{tot}} \ll 1 \implies \mathbb{E}[\cos\Delta\phi_{ij} \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 0$), the continuous-time limit ($2\epsilon^2/\delta t \to \sigma^2$) is the multi-allelic **Wright–Fisher diffusion** on the simplex $\Delta_{N-1}$, $ds_i = \sigma\sum_{j \neq i}\sqrt{s_i s_j}\,dW_{ij}$ ($dW_{ji} = -dW_{ij}$). Crucially, the square-root multiplicative noise coefficient $\sqrt{s_i s_j}$ is not postulated ad hoc—it is the product of the two oscillator amplitudes $|a_i||a_j| = \sqrt{s_i}\sqrt{s_j}$ in the interference cross-term. Because the quadratic variation $d\langle s_i\rangle = \sigma^2 s_i(1-s_i)dt$ vanishes linearly at the boundaries $\{0, 1\}$, Feller's boundary classification guarantees finite-time absorption at a single vertex ($\tau_{\text{game}} < \infty$), and Doob's optional stopping theorem yields $P_i = \mathbb{E}[s_i(\tau_{\text{game}})] = s_i(0)$.
2. **Off-Shell Adler Detuning Against Mode Competition (Theorem 2):** In classical coupled oscillators, the highest-amplitude mode typically locks phase and drains weaker modes ("winner-take-all"). Here, an absorber holding partial energy $e_i = s_i\hbar\omega < \hbar\omega$ is off-shell from its resonant quantum transition by $\Delta E_i = \hbar\omega(1-s_i)$, producing an effective frequency detuning $\Delta_{\text{off}}(s_i) = \omega(1-s_i)$ in Adler's phase-locking equation $\dot{\varphi}_i = \omega(1-s_i) - K\sin\varphi_i$. Outside the narrow natural linewidth layer $1 - s_i > \Gamma/\omega$, $\Delta_{\text{off}} > K$, keeping the oscillator outside the Arnold tongue so coherent drift averages to zero.
3. **Non-Random Vacuum Cross-Term Compensation in Bandgap-Gated Absorbers (Theorem 3):** In semiconductor/superconducting detectors where interband commitment is gated at $\theta = E_{\text{gap}}/\hbar\omega < 1$, threshold gating biases selection toward brighter sites ($p \approx 2.21$). Including a small in-phase reactive vacuum component $\delta b_i = \delta b_i^{\text{rand}} + \epsilon_{\text{vac}}e^{i\phi_i}$ produces a concave deterministic increment $+2\epsilon_{\text{vac}}\sqrt{s_i}dt$, whose simplex projection $ds_1^{\text{vac}} = \tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}}\sqrt{s_1 s_2}(\sqrt{s_2} - \sqrt{s_1})dt$ preferentially lifts dimmer sites and restores $p \approx 2.03$.

## Level 3 — For a Specialist

- **What is genuinely new:** Unlike Hilbert-space state-reduction martingales [Pearle 1976, 1982; Adler et al. 2001], where the state vector $|\psi(t)\rangle$ undergoes stochastic diffusion driven by an external Wiener process coupled to non-commuting operators, the state variable here is the **real mechanical oscillator energy share vector** $\mathbf{s}(t) \in \Delta_{N-1}$ deposited across detector absorber sites after standard wave propagation. The Wright–Fisher diffusion kernel $\sqrt{s_i s_j}$ arises directly from the bilinear complex field-matter interference cross-term $2\operatorname{Re}(a_i^* a_j e^{i\xi_{ij}})$, and the boundary-exact discrete microsimulation algorithm (§4.1) eliminates boundary-clipping bias without altering the first or second moments on $[0, 1]$.
- **What is contested / owed:**
  1. *Nonlocal shared ledger across Macroscopic Spacelike Separation:* When candidate absorber sites $i, j$ lie in opposite arms of a beam splitter [Grangier et al. 1986] or in an entangled pair (§5), conservation of the one-quantum simplex $\sum_i s_i(t) = 1$ and shared-phase pinning require a nonlocal common wave ledger across the coherence volume. While §5 shows that no-signaling holds via $\frac{1}{2}\cos^2(a-b) + \frac{1}{2}\sin^2(a-b) = \frac{1}{2}$, deriving the relativistic covariance of that shared phase ledger requires a spacetime/vacuum completion.
  2. *Parameter sensitivity of $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}}$ in Theorem 3:* While $\theta = 1$ (discrete-level absorbers) is completely parameter-free ($p = 2.001$), cancelling the sub-threshold gate bias at $\theta = 0.72, \lambda = 0.04$ requires $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}} \approx 0.018$ (2-site) and $0.012$ (10-site). A first-principles QED calculation of $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac}}(\theta, \lambda, N)$ from the detector's dielectric susceptibility is owed.

## Key References, Explained

- **Pearle (1976, 1982)** & **Adler, Brody, Brun, & Hughston (2001)** — Established that bounded martingales with variance proportional to $s_i(1-s_i)$ reach $\{0, 1\}$ in finite time with exit probability $s_i(0)$. Cited to credit the mathematical martingale mechanism while contrasting their Hilbert-space state-vector setting with our physical detector-site energy exchange.
- **Lamb & Scully (1969)** — Showed that the photoelectric effect can be modeled by treating the radiation field classically up to the point where detector atoms are driven as quantum/mechanical oscillators. Cited in Postulate 1 for Stage-1 deterministic wave capture.
- **Hahn (1950)**, **Kurnit et al. (1964)**, & **de Riedmatten et al. (2008)** — Experimental demonstrations of spin echoes, photon echoes, and single-photon atomic-frequency-comb memories, proving that initial wave capture in an absorber ensemble is reversible and non-committing prior to Stage-2 irreversible readout.
- **Adler, R. (1946)** — Derived the phase-locking differential equation $\dot{\varphi} = \Delta\omega - K\sin\varphi$ for coupled oscillators. Used in Theorem 2 to prove that off-shell energy deficit $\Delta_{\text{off}} = \omega(1-s_i) > K$ keeps sub-threshold absorber sites outside the Arnold locking tongue.
- **Grangier, Roger, & Aspect (1986)** — Experimental demonstration of single-photon anticorrelation at a beam splitter. Cited to motivate why the conserved simplex $\sum_i s_i = 1$ must span spatially separated output arms.
- **Rui et al. (2020)** & **Srakaew et al. (2023)** — Experimental realizations of sub-wavelength 2D cold-atom arrays ($d < \lambda$) with strong cooperative radiative coupling. Cited in §5.4 (Test E1) as the experimental platform for the port-mismatch quadratic bow test.
