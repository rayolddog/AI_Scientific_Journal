## Substantive findings

1. **The quantum-circuit comparison does not establish the claimed physical implementation (§4.6).** Regime C chooses gates using the simulator's exact current amplitudes and phases. Each chosen matrix can be unitary, while the rule selecting it remains dependent on the input state; preserving the norm does not establish a fixed linear quantum evolution. The manuscript acknowledges amplitude access, but its proposed weak-monitoring implementation still needs an explicit measurement model, measurement backaction, and feedback law that reproduce these trajectories. Regime A simply applies the exact inverse of each known noise kick, which also does not demonstrate a general quantum error-correction scheme.

2. **The “state-independent” control is actually state dependent (§4.6).** Regime B uses an angle proportional to the cosine of the current relative phase, and its gate construction uses the component phases. Calling this a state-independent linear iSWAP control is inaccurate. This undermines the interpretation of the comparison between B and C.

3. **Bell correlations are assumed, then recovered (§§5.1–5.2).** Section 5.1 candidly postulates the conditional Malus law. Section 5.2 nevertheless presents the resulting Bell correlation as generated by Pythagorean conservation. The algebra correctly recovers the correlation and uniform marginals given that conditional law. It does not independently derive the law, a joint detector dynamics, or general relativistic no-signaling. Likewise, the piecewise-linear correlation shown for classical models describes a particular model, not every local hidden-variable theory.

4. **The phase-seeding expression has a concrete periodicity defect.** Both atom and lattice programs use a factor containing `cos[phi_det − (phi_inc + phi_vac)/2]`. Adding 2π to the incident phase represents the same complex incident wave but reverses this cosine's sign. In a simple checked example with modulation strength 0.15 and initially zero phases, the seed factor changes from **1.15 to 0.85**. The authors need a circularly well-defined coupling or an explicit physical justification for additional phase-branch information. Uniform random detector phases may conceal this defect in ensemble statistics; that does not resolve its event-level meaning.

5. **The simulations provide limited evidence for vacuum phase pulling (§§2.2, 4.5).** As the manuscript itself now explains, full-circle uniform phase noise erases the pulling influence on subsequent exchange signs. Agreement with Born frequencies therefore primarily tests the constructed fair exchange process and its initialization. It supplies little discriminatory evidence for the proposed vacuum mechanism. That limitation should govern the abstract and conclusion as well as the ablation discussion.

6. **Threshold agreement remains conditional and parameter dependent (§§3.3–4.5).** Starting every atom below threshold removes immediate commitment, but does not establish unbiased later registration. The disclosed exponent shifts and unequal-subdivision residuals are substantive results. Failure to reject the nine-point Born null is not evidence of exact agreement. A more convincing validation would fix parameters in advance and test new thresholds, atom counts, geometries, and random seeds.
