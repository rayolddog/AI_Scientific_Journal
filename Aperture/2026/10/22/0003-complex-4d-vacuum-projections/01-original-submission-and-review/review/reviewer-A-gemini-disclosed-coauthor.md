By contrast, Thomas–Wigner precession along a non-collinear boost loop $\mathcal{C}_{\text{boost}}$ acts as an **$SU(2)$ spin rotation** $\exp(-i\Omega_{\text{boost}}\mathbf{n}_{\text{loop}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_B/2)$ that rotates $\lvert\uparrow\rangle_B$ and $\lvert\downarrow\rangle_B$ with *opposite* signs ($e^{\pm i\Omega_{\text{boost}}/2}$), transforming the singlet $\lvert\Psi^-\rangle$ into a linear combination of singlet and triplet states! Therefore, an overall scalar Aharonov–Bohm flux $\Phi_B$ on a single trajectory **cannot** cancel an $SU(2)$ Thomas spin precession in a Bell spin-correlation measurement unless the interferometer is a *spatial spin-dependent Stern–Gerlach loop* (where $\lvert\uparrow\rangle_B$ and $\lvert\downarrow\rangle_B$ enclose opposite magnetic fluxes).
  2. *Factor of 2 in $\frac{2e}{\hbar c}$:* For a single electron of charge $q = -e$ (meaning $J = 1$ in Eq. 27), the minimal coupling in Eq. (27) is $\frac{\hbar}{2}J\left(\dot{\chi} + \frac{2e}{\hbar c}A_\mu\dot{x}^\mu\right) = \frac{\hbar}{2}\dot{\chi} + \frac{e}{c}A_\mu\dot{x}^\mu$. Because $\chi \in [0, 4\pi)$ enters the spinor as $e^{i\chi/2}$ (Eq. 7), a shift $\Delta\chi$ produces a quantum mechanical wavefunction phase $\Delta\phi_{\text{QM}} = \Delta\chi / 2$. Equation (50) equates $\oint (1-\cos\theta)d\varphi$ with $\frac{2e}{\hbar c}\Phi_B$, double-counting the factor of 2 relative to the single-electron flux quantum $\Phi_0 = hc/e$.
* **What would fix it:** Reformulate Experiment 2 as a **spin-path (Stern–Gerlach / Mach–Zehnder) entanglement interferometer** where the two spin components $\lvert\uparrow\rangle_B, \lvert\downarrow\rangle_B$ of Particle $B$ are coherently split around a magnetic flux $\Phi_B$ so that the Aharonov–Bohm phase acts in the relative $\sigma_z$ spin channel, matching the $SU(2)$ generator of the Thomas–Wigner boost loop, and verify the factor of $1/2$ between $\Delta\chi$ and wavefunction phase.
